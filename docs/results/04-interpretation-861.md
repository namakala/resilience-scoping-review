# Effective Problem Solving and Caregiver Facilitative Processes in Stressor Resolution and Mastery Development

The capacity to solve problems and manage stressors does not reside solely within the individual but is deeply intertwined with the quality of support provided by caregivers and the broader social environment. Successful problem resolution builds psychological resources and fosters a sense of mastery, whereas repeated failure erodes self-efficacy and reinforces helplessness. Caregivers contribute to this process through facilitative behaviors that buffer adversity and model adaptive coping. The mechanisms through which individuals attempt to manage stress range from adaptive problem-solving to maladaptive escape strategies, with the distinction between these pathways determining whether stress exposure leads to growth or deterioration.

## Problem Solving and Maladaptation

Individuals who successfully resolved problems in domains such as employment and romantic relationships experienced measurable increases in mastery and self-efficacy, demonstrating that effective problem-solving generates positive psychological momentum.[^1] Challenge stressors promoted active problem-solving behavior through challenge appraisal, while hindrance stressors were associated with failed attempts and the depletion of coping resources.[^2] Caregivers played a crucial facilitative role: parents who managed family challenges effectively transmitted resilience to their children, and a sense of being able to support family adaptation emerged as a buffer against mental health problems in high-stress households.[^3] On the maladaptive side, coping mechanisms including problematic online gaming, alcohol consumption, and social media overuse represented attempts to escape negative affect that ultimately amplified the original distress.[^4] However, some evidence complicates this picture: depressive symptoms triggered by stressful life events created a self-perpetuating cycle that persisted beyond the initial stressor, and alcohol consumption as a coping technique provided only short-term mood regulation while ultimately worsening mental and physical health.[^5] Dysfunctional coping skills including behavioural disengagement, self-blame, and passive fantasizing were consistently associated with increased suicidal ideation, whereas productive coping and active reframing were protective.[^6] This dual pattern indicates that the same stressful circumstances can produce divergent trajectories depending on the availability of effective problem-solving skills and supportive caregiving relationships.

## Cross-Theme Synthesis

The results position problem-solving capacity and caregiver facilitation as interdependent determinants of stress resolution. Personal mastery develops through successful problem resolution, but the likelihood of success is itself influenced by the quality of environmental support. Maladaptive coping emerges as a default pathway when effective problem-solving skills are absent and caregiver support is insufficient. Interventions designed to build problem-solving skills while simultaneously strengthening caregiver facilitation may therefore produce synergistic effects that neither approach could achieve independently.
